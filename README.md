# Osteosarcoma-lung epithelial interactions
Metastasis-initiating osteosarcoma subpopulations establish paracrine interactions with both lung and tumor cells to create a metastatic niche
## Project
The goal of this project is to dissect the cellular and molecular mechanisms driving osteosarcoma lung metastasis at the single cell level. This project aimed to understand how a small subpopulation of metastasis-competent tumor cells can orchestrate a reaction in response to the lung that recruits other tumor cells to the lung to proliferate and grow into large metastases. The findings of this project are explained in this graphical abstract and can be found in this pre-print publication (https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.06.09.597967).
![graphical abstract](/abstract_img.png)
## Samples
The samples used to generate single cell libraries consist of: a novel organotypic co-culture system of human osteosarcoma and human bronchioepithelial cells, human xenograft orthotopic and experimental metastasis models, and patient primary and metastatic tissue samples.
## Analysis
The analysis performed here discerned ligand-receptor interactions by osteosarcoma cells within the lung enviroment (NicheNet), identified both transcriptional outputs and relative expression of receptors in response to ligands in the lung environment (IL1R1/IL6/CXCL8 expression), and evaluated cell cycle and activity of gene sets within xenograft and human patient single cell samples (AUCell analysis for NFKB activity)
